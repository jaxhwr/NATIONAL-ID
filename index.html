01 — Executive Summary

Build the foundation once. Connect passports first.

The Republic of the Congo faces a problem common across the region: identity cannot be reliably verified. This drives passport fraud, slows public services, lets public funds leak through duplicate and fictitious beneficiaries, and erodes confidence in the state.

This concept proposes a foundational digital identity system — a single, secure, verified identity for each resident, established once and reused by separate government services. Passports plug in first, because passport fraud is the most visible and immediate pain point, and the one the Ministry of the Interior is currently prioritizing publicly. The model reduces fraud and recovers real money for the state from day one.

Foundational identity register

One de-duplicated, biometrically verified identity per person — the trusted source every service checks against.

Anti-fraud passport module

Uses the identity layer to eliminate duplicate and fraudulent applications before they're ever issued.

Per-module person lookup

An officer pulls up an individual and sees the information relevant to that service, instantly.

Ministry oversight dashboards

Clear, real-time visibility into volumes, processing times, and fraud caught across the country.

AI assistant — typed or spoken

Ask questions in natural language and get answers back as text, tables, or charts, in the form you request.

Citizen verification loop

When the bureau needs more, the system notifies the person to confirm identity or supply missing data securely.

The opportunity in one line

Build the trusted identity foundation first, connect passports immediately, give the Ministry real visibility, and recover measurable money for the state by cutting fraud and eliminating duplicate beneficiaries — a system citizens benefit from and funders will pay for.

03 — The Problem

Three problems, one root cause.

Each of these traces back to a single gap — the absence of a reliable, verifiable identity for each person.

01

Passport fraud

Without confirming an applicant is who they claim — and isn't already holding a passport under another name — duplicate and fraudulent passports are hard to prevent. Touts exploit the delays.

02

Fiscal leakage

Public funds leak through ghost workers, duplicate or fictitious beneficiaries, and payments to the deceased. It is both a budget problem and a corruption problem.

03

Service friction

Citizens repeatedly fill the same paper forms at clinics, banks, and offices — each institution re-collecting and re-storing the same data, badly.

04 — How It Works

A foundation, and the services that plug into it.

The design separates the foundational identity layer from the functional services that use it — keeping each service simple, secure, and cheaper to launch.

Foundational

The identity layer

Establishes once that a person is a unique, real individual. Holds only the minimum needed to do that — core biographic data and the biometrics used for de-duplication.

Functional

The service modules

Passports first; later, others. Each module asks the foundation "is this a verified, unique person?" and works with the information relevant to that module.

Each module includes a fast, direct lookup — the everyday operational tool that lets staff serve the person in front of them without paperwork or delay. Try it:

05 — The Return

How this makes the country money.

The country earns by running more efficiently and leaking less — and the return aligns perfectly with the Ministry's anti-corruption agenda.

$ →

Plugging fiscal leakage

De-duplication removes ghost workers, duplicate beneficiaries, and payments to the deceased. Typically the single largest, fastest return — and a visible anti-corruption win in year one.

+

Broadening the tax base

Verifiable identity brings more people and businesses into the formal, taxable economy and makes bank KYC possible.

÷

Eliminating duplicated systems

A shared identity layer means every new service is cheaper to launch — a direct saving the finance ministry understands.

⚡

Faster service delivery

Less paper, fewer in-person trips, faster passport issuance, lower administrative cost per transaction.

✓

Restored passport integrity

Ending trafficking protects legitimate passport-fee revenue and restores the credibility of the national travel document.

The strategic point

Fiscal leakage is money the state is already losing every month. A system that recovers even a fraction of it can pay for itself many times over — while simultaneously delivering the Minister's anti-corruption and passport-reform promises. Efficiency and integrity are the revenue engine; the data's value is realized as savings and recovered funds, not as a commodity to be sold.

06 — Roadmap & Next Steps

Phase One is the foundation and passports.

The highest-impact, most visible win. Later phases connect additional services — records, healthcare check-in, bank KYC — to the same foundation, each as a separate decision, so value compounds without rebuilding identity each time.

1

Confirm the current state of civil registration

Is it digital or paper? This determines whether Phase One is "modernize" or "build from zero" — the first question any funder or partner asks.

2

Align partners on scope, roles, and contribution

Set the shape of the partnership before approaching the Ministry formally.

3

Open an exploratory conversation on financing

For example, the World Bank's ID4D initiative or the African Development Bank, which fund exactly these systems.

4

Develop the technical architecture and cost model

Once the above are known, scope the Phase One build in detail.
